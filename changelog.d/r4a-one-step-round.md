- A reply no longer stops while the prompt of a request that came in is run: on the models the description executor serves several sequences of (Gemma 4, Qwen3.8 dense, Muse Spark, Kimi-K3, GLM-5.3-Flash), a round of the scheduler is one step of the replies' next tokens and the prompts' chunks together. On an RTX 4080 with gemma-4-12B and eight requests coming in one after another, the longest gaps between a reply's tokens (the 99th percentile) go from 289 to 82 ms with 1k-token prompts and from 478 to 97 ms with 4k-token ones, at the same tokens/s in all; the first token of a prompt that comes in while others are answered is 8% to 10% later (benchmarks/gpu/rtx4080-one-step-round-2026-11-22). A token that is made beside a long prompt chunk can differ in its last bits from the same token made alone. `GLYD_ENGINE_ONE_STEP=0` gives the earlier round, and `GLYD_ENGINE_STEP_TOKENS=64` keeps a step small enough that it cannot.
- `glyd-engine bench` has loads whose requests begin one after another (`--loads 4k+250ms:8`, `--suite arrive`), which is where a server's handling of prompts that come in under load shows.
