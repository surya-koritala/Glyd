- The planner prices a step of many users through the expert cache by the distinct experts the step misses, each read once, where it had priced one user's misses at any number of users. Checked on the recorded decode routing of Nemotron 3 Super (20 generations side by side, 1 to 20 at a time, caches of 17, 32 and 46% of the experts): the planner's missed experts a step are within 19% of the cache's at every point (before, 7.4 a layer at 20 users against 215).
- At many users a step's experts are placed busiest last, so that they stay on the GPU for the next step: on the same routing, 1 to 7 points more of the experts' uses are found on the GPU at 16 and 20 users, and never fewer.
- `glyd-engine ceiling --stats` follows the expert cache's queues layer by layer (the copies of a layer wait behind the previous layer's promotions, which wait for its products; a step copied whole has the CPU wait for it). On the RTX 4080 SUPER's measured runs of random DeepSeek-V4 and Kimi-K3 at the release's widths, calibrated on each model's copy run, it predicts the auto runs at 1 and 8 users within 7% (DeepSeek-V4 at 8 users: -6.8%, where it was -13.4%). `storebench --out` writes each layer's totals, with the calls copied whole.
- Development builds can plan the expert cache's decode steps on the GPU (`--device-plan`; `tiercheck` and `storebench --device-plan`): for DeepSeek-V4's and Kimi-K3's FP4 experts, decode calls of up to 8 users split their misses between copies and a CPU thread as the CPU's plan does, with no CPU wait inside a step, so a decode step is one CUDA graph (checked on an A10 with random models). Its speed against the CPU's plan is not measured yet. Part of #327.
