- CUDA graphs for more of a served model's steps on the description executor. Decode steps of several users on Qwen3.5 and Nemotron-H now replay a graph of their rows padded to 2, 4, 8, ..., as Qwen3, Gemma 4 and Muse Spark already did: the gated delta rule's and Mamba-2's convolution and recurrence take a step's rows in one launch, each row through its own state, with each row's bits those of its own pass. On an A10, 8 users' tokens/s per user on Qwen3.5-0.8B: 230.7 to 292.4 with short prompts, 165.5 to 194.5 with 1k prompts. Full prompt and mixed steps (a chunk's rows: long prompts, alone or beside decode rows) run in pieces: the products, norms and experts between two attention or recurrent layers are one graph each, captured when the server starts, and the attention runs eager between them, so the host queues one launch a piece where it queued every kernel. A replay is the eager pass bit for bit (pieces are captured for a pass's exact rows, not padded); decode steps of models whose steps take no whole graph (Kimi-K3, GLM-5.3-Flash, DeepSeek-V4, Muse Spark's picked attention, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next) run in pieces too, at 2 to 16, 32 and 64 rows, captured at start; passes of other sizes, and passes whose products take cuBLAS (the A10's and A100's prompt passes, where pieces cost 0.7% of a full pass's GPU time on gemma-4-12B), run eager. `GLYD_PROF=1` prints the piece graphs replayed a pass; `GLYD_ENGINE_PIECES=0` (a development build's) runs such passes eager. A step's metadata (positions, the decode rows' slots and their states' addresses) is written to the GPU in one copy, which every graph reads. Part of #329.
