- A plan never promises more tokens than the pool it sizes holds, with bf16 pages or the lossless 12-bit pages. The planner's 12-bit bytes per token are now the pool's: a sixteenth of the page plus its escape room and its share of the raw slabs, 1.5% to 3.1% more than before (`kv12check` now requires the planner's figure to be at least the pages' own and at most 1% above it). The pool now gives each page exactly that escape room, set by the page's shape, instead of twice what the calibration counted. A page whose values need more room is made raw, and the newest sequence is paused where raw slabs run short. Plans now also count, once for all users, what the 12-bit pages' pools spend as the context grows: a raw slab for every page of one sequence, which lets a sequence alone always fit, and the bf16 rows the pages are unpacked into. This costs up to about half of one sequence's 12-bit pages at long contexts. For one user, 12-bit pages now plan fewer tokens than bf16 pages (gemma-4-12B on an L4: 257,824 against 298,496; eight users: 19,680 against 10,144). Gemma 4's plans size a sliding layer's ring in whole blocks, as the pools give them out. When the pools' budget falls short of every user's context, they now hold one sequence's blocks in every group first, so one sequence holds the context its plan gives it. Part of #332.
- No two runs of one 12-bit KV write fill one page at once. A run whose page another run of the same write names now writes nothing, and the status counts it and names the page (words 14 and 15 of `glyd_gpu_kv_write`'s status). The engine then fails the pass with an error that names the block. A pass's rows past the blocks their sequence holds are not written, so block 0, which belongs to no sequence, is never written. Before, the long-context check grew a sequence once and wrote its later prompt chunks' sliding-window rows into block 0, several runs to a call. Those writes took escape chunks and raw slabs that no page named, until a later step found no slab and failed ("no block left"); it did so on some runs and not others. The check now grows a sequence before each pass, as the scheduler does. `kvshapes` makes such a colliding write on purpose and checks that both runs are refused and that nothing of the pools is lost. `usercheck --ctx plan --kv12` plans with the 12-bit pages (`GLYD_KV=lossless`). Part of #332.
